Button ladder on GPIO14 (6 buttons on one wire)

A 10k resistor pulls the GPIO14 node up to 3V3. Pressing a button connects the node to GND through that button’s resistor, so each button puts a different voltage on GPIO14. The shutter has no resistor, so it pulls the node straight to 0V.

What the pinout told us (and what I changed because of it)

  • GPIO48 is the on-board RGB LED, so I no longer use it for the screen. Driving the screen from it would make that LED flicker. The screen’s DC line moved to GPIO41.
  • GPIO2 drives the small on-board LED (IO2). That’s why it now runs the flash transistor, and the little LED will blink with the flash. Nothing else uses it, so it can’t get dimmed by a button.
  • GPIO0 is the BOOT button pin. Select uses it, so don’t hold Select while you press Reset or plug in power, or the board will enter download mode.
  • GPIO3 is a strapping pin (JTAG enable). It is fine for the Next button as long as you don’t hold Next while resetting.
  • GPIO38, 39 and 40 are the on-board SD slot and GPIO35 to 37 are the PSRAM. We use none of them, and the camera pins (4 to 18 except 14) stay untouched.
  • GPIO1 and GPIO14 are plain analog pins with nothing else attached. That’s why the pot and the button ladder sit there. GPIO43, 44, 19 and 20 are left alone for the serial and USB ports.
  • The header shows only one GND pin. Run a short, thick wire from it to your GND bus, and don’t rely on it for more than the ESP itself.
